## Limitations that apply to all insights

- Return/Repair is a selected population (machines that were returned or repaired). Differences describe that group against the Normal group and are not estimates of what would happen to a typical machine. Return reasons and dates are not in the data.
- Small samples: 36 RR machines (59 SV53 Normal in rate comparisons, 54 Normal with motor data). Many comparisons were made in Steps 6 and 6b, so no single interval is proof; there is no multiplicity correction.
- Correlation only; no causal claim is made or implied.
- Normal and RR differ in model mix, observation window length and calendar coverage; these are only partly controlled (SV53 restriction, ROW-PROD, SV53-AB and common-month checks).
- Fault timestamps and session dates are on different clocks (server receipt dates trail event times by hours to weeks), so linking a fault to a session is approximate.
- Fault rates treat "absent from the faults table" as zero faults (assumed). `fault_status`, `hex_error_code`, `log_type` and the time-sync columns have no stated meaning and are not used for any claim; the status-rule count is a sensitivity check only.
- Fault type/value meanings come from the brief (types 11, 12, 13 only occur). Treating Filter Wash 13/1 as a maintenance alert is our grouping.
- Data quality: after removing exact copies, re-uploads and empty payloads, 14% of raw fault rows and 3% of raw battery rows remain as analysis rows (`dq_pct_kept_faults_all`, `dq_pct_kept_battery_all`); 78% of raw fault rows are exact copies not kept (`dq_exact_copy_faults_all`) and 95% of raw battery rows have no payload (`dq_empty_battery_all`). The battery data in particular is too thin for cohort comparison.
- Cumulative counters (motor) are converted to per-period deltas; periods with a counter decrease are excluded rather than treated as resets.

## I1. Filter Wash reminders (13/1) flag and precede airway blockages (type 12)

- **What:** sessions with a Filter Wash alert are far more likely to record an airway blockage, and blockage episodes are more frequent in the days after an alert. **Where:** Normal SV50 (87 to 105 machines depending on the test), Normal SV53 (25 to 68), RR SV53 (18 to 43). **When:** Oct 2025 to Jun 2026. **How:** (a) same session: Mantel-Haenszel risk ratio with strata = machine (`f_i1_samesession_rr_*`); (b) lead: type-12 episode rate in the window after a 13/1 vs outside, Mantel-Haenszel with strata = machine x calendar month, exposure span from the sessions table (`f_i1_lead_adjratio_*`). **Why:** a leading marker is the most actionable pattern in the data.
- **Numbers:** same session, all groups 6.0x [4.3 to 8.6] (130 machines); Normal SV50 4.8x [3.1 to 7.9], Normal SV53 16.6x [10.4 to 31.8], RR SV53 7.1x [5.1 to 18.3]; leave-one-machine-out minimum 5.7, 4.4, 14.2, 6.6. In sessions with both, the alert came first in 68%. Lead, 7 days, time-adjusted: 2.8x [1.8 to 4.4] (74 machines), 3.0x [1.7 to 4.9] (17), 3.4x [1.7 to 6.5] (16); leave-one-out minimum 2.6, 2.6, 2.8. Starting 1 hour later: 1.7x [1.1 to 2.6], 1.6x [0.7 to 3.2], 2.8x [1.5 to 5.2]. Over 30 days: 1.7x [0.8 to 3.6], 1.2x [0.6 to 2.5], 2.1x [1.0 to 3.9]. Under the first, faults-only exposure span (7 days): 1.7x [1.0 to 3.0] (40), 2.5x [0.8 to 6.0] (7), 2.9x [1.5 to 5.6] (7).
- **Choices made during analysis (not pre-registered):** main window 7 days; sensitivity 30 days and a 1-hour lagged start; a machine needs at least 1 day inside and 14 days outside the windows (added after a first run without them was unstable); events less than 1 hour apart are one episode; link window 24 h. All in `f_i1_param_*`; the full table is `reports/insights/f_i1_robustness.csv`.
- **Flags (interval touches or includes 1):** lagged 7-day, Normal SV53; 30-day, Normal SV50, Normal SV53 and RR (lower bound 1.01); faults-only span, Normal SV50 (lower bound 1.0) and Normal SV53. Same-session ratios never touch 1.
- **Confidence:** same-session association: high. Lead after a reminder: moderate; about 3x in the 7-day window in every group and robust to dropping any one machine, but smaller once the alert's own session is excluded and not distinguishable from 1 in some groups over 30 days.
- **Caveats:** order uses `fault_timestamp` (device time, inferred); the alert may be a symptom of the same restriction as the blockage [inferred]; exposure span from the sessions table is a labelled deviation from the faults-only design, which is unstable (7 machines in two groups); Return/Repair is a selected population.
- **Decision at Dyson:** treat a Filter Wash alert as an early warning of a likely blockage (firmer prompt, service or care message) and check with the firmware owner whether the two alerts share a trigger.

## I2. SV50 telemetry gaps and battery heat

- **What:** SV50 is missing fields that SV53 reports, and the battery heat it does report is concentrated. **Where:** Normal SV50 (105 machines, 19,124 sessions; 15 with battery data) vs SV53 (111 machines; 42 with battery data: 7 Normal, 35 RR). **When:** Oct 2025 to Jun 2026. **How:** counts and shares of analysis sessions (`f_i2_notrep_*`, `f_i2_dust_*`, `f_i2_ctx_*`); share of discharge seconds above 45 C from `dischargeBandBins` (`f_i2_dis_hot_*`, `f_i2_any_hot_*`, `f_i2_ratio_*`). **Why:** it changes how model comparisons on battery and dust must be read.
- **Numbers:** no temperature flag in 74.1% [70.3 to 77.2] of SV50 sessions vs 2.7% [1.5 to 4.2] (Normal SV53) and 3.2% [1.1 to 5.8] (RR SV53); model difference 71.4 pp [67.3 to 74.7], cohort difference within SV53 0.5 pp [-2.1 to 3.3]. SV50 dust: 0% of sessions (SV53 73%). No SV50 machine has motor rows (0 of 105). Discharge time above 45 C: SV50 2.99% [0.18 to 7.68], SV53 0.06% [0.01 to 0.14]; any such time on 9 of 15 SV50 vs 5 of 42 SV53 machines (Fisher p 0.0006); the top SV50 machine holds 85% of SV50 hot time, and without it SV50 is 0.53% [0.08 to 1.34], 8.8x SV53 [1.2 to 98]. Only 7 sessions (4 SV50 machines) are flagged HOT, none COLD.
- **Confidence:** high for the gaps (counts and a 71 pp model contrast); moderate for the heat result (15 SV50 machines, one dominates, wide interval).
- **Caveats:** the meaning of the no-flag state is not stated; firmware is recorded in under 2% of sessions so it cannot be tested as a driver; heat exposure shows no cause; SV50 packs are 4-cell and SV53 7-cell, with different power profiles.
- **Decision at Dyson:** ask the SV50 firmware and BMS owners why temperature flags, dust values and motor logs are missing, and do not compare models on those fields until fixed; look at the SV50 machines with heat exposure above 45 C.

## I3. Blockage concentrates in sub-model SV53-AB; the RR vs Normal gap shrinks after re-weighting

- **What:** within Normal SV53, SV53-AB logs far more blockages than the other sub-models; part of the RR excess is sub-model mix. **Where:** rate-eligible SV53 machines (Normal 59, RR 36). **When:** Oct 2025 to Jun 2026. **How:** pooled rates per 100 sessions by sub-model (`f_i3_rate12_*`); direct standardisation of AB, AC, AE and AF to the **Normal** share of sessions (weights AB 23.6%, AC 12.7%, AE 18.7%, AF 45.0%; `f_i3_std_weight_*`), SV53-AA left out; ratio with machine bootstrap of both cohorts. **Why:** it points reliability work at a sub-model and corrects the earlier headline.
- **Numbers (machines):** Normal SV53-AB 8.2 [1.2 to 18.7] (18); AC 0.6 [0.2 to 1.5] (4); AE 2.3 [1.2 to 3.2] (5); AF 1.0 [0.5 to 2.0] (30). RR SV53-AB 17.0 [4.9 to 41.3] (21); AF 9.9 [4.0 to 22.3] (11); AC and AE have 2 machines each. AB minus the other Normal sub-models 6.9 [0.05 to 17.6], permutation p 0.006. AB is 31% of Normal and 58% of RR machines. RR / Normal: 4.7x [1.5 to 14.1] as recorded, 3.1x [1.3 to 9.3] re-weighted. Inside SV53-AB, RR minus Normal is 8.9 [-8.2 to 31.7], and -0.4 [-11.1 to 8.5] without the RR machine with most events (45% of RR blockage events). SV50-AA is also high (10.5 vs 4.2 for AF; p 0.10).
- **Confidence:** moderate for the SV53-AB concentration (lower bound almost 0, p about 0.01, many cuts); low for the size of the re-weighting effect (the two ratios' intervals overlap, so the fall from 4.7 to 3.1 is not a tested difference).
- **Caveats:** sub-model codes have no stated meaning; RR AF is as high as RR AB, so the gradient is a Normal-population result; one machine drives RR.
- **Decision at Dyson:** focus blockage investigation on SV53-AB (and look at SV50-AA), and do not quote a single RR vs Normal multiple without the sub-model mix.

## I4. Blockage escalation by model (Normal machines, so models are compared on one cohort)

- **What:** how a "motor still running" blockage warning (12/1) turns into an error (12/2). **Where:** Normal SV50 (77 machines with a 12/1 session) vs Normal SV53 (21). **When:** Oct 2025 to Jun 2026. **How:** session = serial + session_id linked on matching server dates; order from `fault_timestamp`; machine bootstraps (`f_i4_*`); SV53 pooled over cohorts is only a supplementary row. **Why:** it shows how long a user has between warning and stall.
- **Timestamps:** `fault_timestamp` has fractional seconds in the raw file (e.g. 18:01:17.950000); 99.3% of values are not whole seconds, 67.5% are multiples of 10 ms, and the smallest positive gap between two faults of one machine is 0.086 s (`f_i4_ts_*`). That resolves the seconds measured here (median 3.8 s). Of sessions with both, none have identical 12/1 and 12/2 timestamps (SV50, SV53), and 6 of 148 SV50 sessions have the error timestamped first (excluded from the gap). They are device time (inferred).
- **Numbers:** warning sessions that also record an error: SV50 47% [36 to 58], SV53 34% [18 to 46]; difference 13.4 pp [-2.9 to 32.0] (not distinguishable). Median seconds from warning to error: SV50 3.8 [3.6 to 4.2] (47 machines), SV53 4.6 [3.8 to 11.2] (8 machines); difference -0.8 s [-7.5 to 0.2]. Error sessions with an earlier warning: SV50 92% [88 to 96], SV53 27% [11 to 44]; difference 65.2 pp [47.5 to 82.5]; pooled SV53 70.3 pp [57.9 to 82.9].
- **Confidence:** high for the model difference in whether an error is preceded by a warning; low for escalation share and timing (SV53 is small and the intervals overlap).
- **Caveats:** SV53 has 8 machines with a measurable gap; counts are sessions; the SV53 logs may skip the warning stage in logging or in behaviour [not testable here].
- **Decision at Dyson:** SV50 gives a warning stage of a few seconds before a stall; SV53 mostly does not log one. Ask the firmware owner whether SV53 skips the warning, and whether a few seconds is enough for any protective action.
