# Insight cards (Step 7)

Generated from `src/insights.py` (called by `notebooks/02_insights.ipynb`). Every number below is a row in `reports/insights/key_numbers.csv`; ids are given in brackets. Machines are the unit. `key_numbers.csv` has the numerator in `n_events_or_sessions` and the denominator with its unit in `n_denominator` / `denominator_unit` for each rate. Fault rates are pooled (total faults / total sessions x 100) with 95% bootstrap intervals from resampling machines within each group (2,000 resamples, fixed seed); continuous measures are machine-level medians. Ratios are rounded ("about 5x"). **All results are exploratory. Return/Repair (RR) is a selected population, and nothing here states or implies a cause.** Filter Wash (13/1) as a "maintenance alert" is **our analytical grouping, not the brief's**; the brief only says fault type 13 is Filter Wash, value 1 "needs cleaning".

Interval notation: rates carry the interval of the pooled rate; differences carry the interval of the difference (RR minus Normal, or SV53 minus SV50).

## Insight 1: Filter Wash alerts account for most of the SV50 vs SV53 fault-rate gap

- **Headline:** Within Normal machines, SV50 log 16.5 faults per 100 clean sessions against 4.3 for SV53, and Filter Wash (13/1) is about 81% of that gap, so cohort comparisons of total fault counts need like-for-like models.
- **Chart:** `reports/figures/final/02_insight1_model_effect_fault_rate.png` (and `.svg`)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Normal SV50 (99 machines): total 16.5 [12.6 to 21.7]; Filter Wash 11.4 [8.7 to 15.0]; all other 5.1 [3.4 to 7.5] (`i1_total_normal_SV50`, `i1_fw_normal_SV50`, `i1_other_normal_SV50`)
  - Normal SV53 (59 machines): total 4.3 [2.0 to 7.5]; Filter Wash 1.5 [0.5 to 3.0]; all other 2.8 [1.2 to 5.3] (`i1_total_normal_SV53`, `i1_fw_normal_SV53`, `i1_other_normal_SV53`)
  - RR SV53 (36 machines): total 18.7 [7.9 to 37.0]; Filter Wash 5.6 [2.0 to 10.9]; all other 13.1 [5.1 to 26.7] (`i1_total_return_repair_SV53`, `i1_fw_return_repair_SV53`, `i1_other_return_repair_SV53`)
  - Gap, Normal SV53 minus SV50: all faults -12.2 [-17.9 to -7.3]; Filter Wash -10.0 [-13.5 to -6.9]; all other -2.3 [-5.3 to 0.7] (`i1_diff_total_normal_sv53_minus_sv50`, `i1_diff_fw_normal_sv53_minus_sv50`, `i1_diff_other_normal_sv53_minus_sv50`); Filter Wash share of the gap 81% (`i1_fw_share_of_gap`)
  - Like-for-like (SV53), RR minus Normal: all faults 14.4 [2.4 to 34.4]; excluding Filter Wash 10.3 [1.7 to 24.7]; Filter Wash alone 4.1 [0.2 to 9.7] (`i1_diff_total_rr_minus_normal_sv53`, `i1_diff_other_rr_minus_normal_sv53`, `i1_diff_fw_rr_minus_normal_sv53`)
- **Robustness (from 6b):** The SV50-vs-SV53 gap in total rate has an interval that excludes 0, and so does the Filter Wash part; excluding Filter Wash the interval spans 0. The direction (SV53 lower) holds for every type/value. In the pooled all-model comparison, RR vs Normal shows 13/1 *lower* in RR (-2.1, interval -6.3 to 4.0, 6b-0) but *higher* under SV53-only: that sign flip is why 13/1 is not used for any RR-vs-Normal claim and why the comparison must be like-for-like.
- **Caveats:** Normal has SV50 machines and RR has none, so an all-machine RR-vs-Normal fault comparison mixes model with cohort (verified from the data: RR rate-eligible machines are all SV53). Only 59 SV53 Normal and 36 RR machines. Intervals are wide for RR. Filter Wash "needs cleaning" is counted as a fault and called a maintenance alert by us; whether SV50 and SV53 trigger the alert under the same conditions is not stated (model difference in alert thresholds is a possible reading, an interpretation we cannot test). Fault timestamps and session dates are on different clocks (session timing is approximate). Sub-model mix within SV53 is only partly controlled.
- **Draft so what (audience: engineering leadership and product):** A raw "faults per session" league table across models mostly reflects Filter Wash volume, which differs about 8x between SV50 and SV53 in Normal machines. Fault KPIs should be reported by model and with and without 13/1.
- **Hedged recommendation:** Validate with the firmware owner how Filter Wash is raised on each model (the alert definition and threshold per model); until then report fault rates per model family. Data that would confirm: the fault code documentation per model, and Filter Wash events with the clean/wash actions that follow.

## Insight 2: Among SV53 machines, Return/Repair log about 5x the airway-blockage rate of Normal

- **Headline:** Among SV53 machines, RR machines recorded 13.1 airway-blockage faults (type 12) per 100 clean sessions against 2.8 for Normal (about 5x), and the gap also appears per hour of motor runtime.
- **Chart:** `reports/figures/final/03_insight2_airway_blockage_sv53.png` (and `.svg`)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Type 12 (values 1+2) per 100 sessions, Normal (59 machines) vs RR (36): 2.8 [1.2 to 5.3] vs 13.1 [5.1 to 26.7]; difference 10.3 [1.7 to 24.7]; ratio 4.7, about 5x (`i2_t12_main_normal`, `i2_t12_main_return_repair`, `i2_diff_t12_main`, `i2_ratio_t12_main`)
  - 12/1 (inlet blocked, motor still running): 1.7 [0.4 to 3.9] vs 6.6 [1.7 to 14.7] (`i2_t12_v1_main_normal`, `i2_t12_v1_main_return_repair`)
  - 12/2 (inlet blocked and in error; the stricter version): 1.1 [0.6 to 1.7] vs 6.5 [2.8 to 12.3]; difference 5.4 [1.5 to 11.5]; about 6x (`i2_t12_v2_main_normal`, `i2_t12_v2_main_return_repair`, `i2_diff_t12_v2_main`, `i2_ratio_t12_v2_main`)
  - Per motor runtime hour (45 Normal / 30 RR machines): type 12 0.32 [0.09 to 0.70] vs 1.73 [0.58 to 3.56]; difference 1.41 [0.17 to 3.12]; about 5x. 12/2 alone 0.09 [0.05 to 0.15] vs 0.87 [0.33 to 1.61]; difference 0.78 [0.23 to 1.51]; about 10x (`i2h_n_t12_win_normal`, `i2h_n_t12_win_return_repair`, `i2h_diff_t12`, `i2h_ratio_t12`, `i2h_n_t12_2_win_normal`, `i2h_n_t12_2_win_return_repair`, `i2h_diff_t12v2`, `i2h_ratio_t12v2`)
- **Robustness (from 6b):** Direction holds in every variant tried. ROW-PROD only (54 vs 35 machines): type 12 2.4 vs 13.1, difference 10.7 [2.0 to 24.5] (`i2_diff_t12_rowprod`). SV53-AB only (18 vs 21): 8.2 vs 17.0, difference 8.9 [-8.6 to 33.8], so the type 12 interval **spans 0** there; 12/2 alone 1.9 vs 7.8, difference 5.9 [0.2 to 15.6], which excludes 0 (`i2_diff_t12_ab`, `i2_diff_t12_v2_ab`). It survives the per-runtime-hour exposure adjustment. Two things are not claimed: 12/1 on its own (its machine-share result flips under SV53-only in Step 6), and a machine-share result for 12/2 (the share of machines with at least one 12/2 has an interval spanning 0, 6b-b). Normal SV53-AB machines have a higher type 12 rate (8.2) than SV53 Normal overall (2.8), so sub-model mix matters.
- **Caveats:** RR is a selected population: machines were returned or repaired for reasons we do not have, so a higher fault rate is what one might expect if faults prompted returns; this cannot tell us whether blockages cause failures. 36 RR machines; intervals are wide, so "about 5x" is a point estimate, not a precise multiple. Pooled rates are dominated by machines with many sessions and faults. Fault-to-session timing is approximate (different clocks). The per-hour result uses faults inside each machine's motor observation window and excludes machines absent from the faults table (not zero-filled); it uses only the 45 / 30 machines with motor data. The meanings of 12/1 and 12/2 are from the brief; `fault_status` meaning is unknown (primary counts do not depend on it; the status-rule sensitivity count gave the same direction in Step 6). Sub-model mix differs by cohort (SV53-AB: 18 Normal vs 21 RR; SV53-AF: 30 vs 11).
- **Draft so what (audience: reliability / quality engineering):** Airway blockage is the fault type that separates returned from normal SV53 machines most consistently, per session and per hour of running. It is a candidate field-signal to review alongside return reasons.
- **Hedged recommendation:** Validate against return/repair reasons (does the repair record mention airway, filter or brush bar?) and against the usage mix (dust load, floor type). Data that would confirm: return reason codes and dates, fault-status semantics, and a larger and unselected comparison sample. Do not set a threshold from these numbers.

## Insight 3: Return/Repair machines run about 2x the motor minutes per day of Normal machines

- **Headline:** The median RR machine ran its motor 4.9 minutes per day against 2.4 for the median Normal machine (about 2x), measured over differing observation windows.
- **Chart:** `reports/figures/final/04_insight3_motor_minutes_per_day.png` (and `.svg`)
- **Key numbers:**
  - Median motor minutes per day: Normal (54 machines) 2.38 [1.78 to 3.36]; RR (36 machines) 4.87 [3.08 to 6.38]; difference 2.49 [0.43 to 3.98]; ratio 2.04, about 2x (`i3_median_main_normal`, `i3_median_main_return_repair`, `i3_diff_main`, `i3_ratio_main`)
  - Observation window, median valid motor days per machine: 240 Normal vs 91 RR (`i3_valid_days_normal`, `i3_valid_days_return_repair`)
  - All 54 + 36 machines are SV53 (checked in code), so no model adjustment is needed here.
- **Robustness (from 6b):** ROW-PROD only (49 vs 35): 2.46 vs 5.05, difference 2.59 [0.29 to 4.07] (`i3_diff_rowprod`). Over the common calendar months 2025-10 to 2026-05: whole periods only, 2.17 vs 4.45, difference 2.28 [0.49 to 3.91] (`i3_diff_window`); pro-rated, 2.29 vs 4.87, difference 2.58 [0.54 to 3.96] (`i3_diff_prorated`). SV53-AB only (15 vs 22): 1.71 vs 4.21, difference 2.50 [-0.45 to 5.38], direction holds but the interval **spans 0** (`i3_diff_ab`); SV53-AB ROW-PROD only (14 vs 22) likewise spans 0 (`i3_diff_ab_rowprod`).
- **Caveats:** Selected population. **Observation windows differ: median valid motor days are 91 for RR vs 240 for Normal** (`i3_valid_days_return_repair`, `i3_valid_days_normal`). **SV53-AB only (22 RR vs 15 Normal machines): 4.21 vs 1.71 min/day, difference 2.50 [-0.45 to 5.38]; the direction holds but the interval spans 0** (`i3_diff_ab`), so the result is not established within one sub-model. Usage in the window before a return may also differ from usage over a machine's life. "per day" divides runtime by elapsed days between weekly snapshots, so idle days count as zero. Motor runtime for RR machines is the sum of the power bins, which we assume equals runtime (verified for Normal only). Periods with a counter decrease are excluded, not treated as resets. Small samples; sub-model mix differs by cohort. The result tells us RR machines were used more in the observed window; it does not tell us usage caused the return, nor that RR machines are used more than Normal over their whole life.
- **Draft so what (audience: product and reliability):** Return/Repair machines are in heavier use, so fault counts should be compared per unit of use (per hour of motor runtime), as in Insight 2, rather than per calendar week.
- **Hedged recommendation:** Validate with a like-for-like window (the same calendar weeks before the return date) once return dates are available, and check whether usage is higher because owners use them more or because of machine-side behaviour (e.g. low suction driving longer cleans); that second reading is an interpretation we cannot test here. Data that would confirm: return dates, return reasons, and the unit of `clean_duration`.

## Limitations that apply to all insights

- Return/Repair is a selected population (machines that were returned or repaired). Differences describe that group against the Normal group and are not estimates of what would happen to a typical machine. Return reasons and dates are not in the data.
- Small samples: 36 RR machines (59 SV53 Normal in rate comparisons, 54 Normal with motor data). Many comparisons were made in Steps 6 and 6b, so no single interval is proof; there is no multiplicity correction.
- Correlation only; no causal claim is made or implied.
- Normal and RR differ in model mix, observation window length and calendar coverage; these are only partly controlled (SV53 restriction, ROW-PROD, SV53-AB and common-month checks).
- Fault timestamps and session dates are on different clocks (server receipt dates trail event times by hours to weeks), so linking a fault to a session is approximate.
- Fault rates treat "absent from the faults table" as zero faults (assumed). `fault_status`, `hex_error_code`, `log_type` and the time-sync columns have no stated meaning and are not used for any claim; the status-rule count is a sensitivity check only.
- Fault type/value meanings come from the brief (types 11, 12, 13 only occur). Treating Filter Wash 13/1 as a maintenance alert is our grouping.
- Data quality: after removing exact copies, re-uploads and empty payloads, 14% of raw fault rows and 3% of raw battery rows remain as analysis rows (`dq_pct_kept_faults_all`, `dq_pct_kept_battery_all`); 78% of raw fault rows are exact copies not kept (`dq_exact_copy_faults_all`) and 95% of raw battery rows have no payload (`dq_empty_battery_all`). The battery data in particular is too thin for cohort comparison.
- Cumulative counters (motor) are converted to per-period deltas; periods with a counter decrease are excluded rather than treated as resets.
